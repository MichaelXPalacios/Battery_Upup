## 3. Task 2: shift factors for the structural and regional constraints (`derived/sf_structural.parquet`, `data/sf_structural*.csv`)

**Method.**
1. *Matrix.* Hours x substations: the median day-ahead price of a substation's resource nodes minus HB_BUSAVG, 669 substations with prices in at least 90% of hours.
2. *Patterns.* Each substation's series is divided by its standard deviation clipped to $2-8 (so one wild node cannot dominate), soft-thresholded at $1/MWh and sign-split into a discount block and a premium block; non-negative matrix factorisation (k = 32, NNDSVDa start) gives non-negative activity and a discount/premium loading per substation. A signed pattern is one with both blocks (a constraint with an export and an import side). PCA/varimax was run first as the dimension check: 8 components explain 91% of the standardised variance, 16 explain 96%, 32 explain 98.6%. k = 32 is a pragmatic point on a slowly rising hold-out curve (half-stations-to-other-half R2 of the regional patterns 0.69 at k = 8, 0.75 at 16, 0.75 at 24, 0.77 at 32, 0.82 at 40), not an elbow; patterns are stable to k +-4 (best-match r 0.82 to 1.00 for the seven retained; the weakest are Bell County at k = 36 and the Panhandle).
3. *Shift factors.* With the pattern activity fixed (the shadow price at the pattern's "pole", the mean of its five largest loadings, so SF = 1 there), each substation's basis is regressed on all 32 activities without an intercept: SF = -coefficient (PLAN.md: cong = -SF x mu). Re-referenced to the median priced station (HB_BUSAVG moves when a regional pattern binds). Loadings below 0.20 are read as zero: the regional patterns are bimodal, and the mid-band is mostly isolated stations (regression noise about 0.1). Clipped to +-1 like Phase 3b. Zone and hub rows come from regressing the LZ_ and HB_ price series on the same activities.
4. *Substations without a priced node* get the inverse-distance **weighted median** of their four nearest priced stations within 60 km (at least two); `method = dam60_factor_interp`, quality tagged `+interp` and `+far` beyond 30 km. A mean let one solar-farm station in Brazos County (WESTEX shift factor 0.71) pull Brenham to 0.16; the median does not. Leave-one-out over the priced stations: correlation 0.75 (Bell County) to 0.93 (WESTEX), right sign at 62% (NELRIO and Bell County, narrow pockets) to 94% (WESTEX) of footprint stations: the median is conservative at small footprints.
5. *Which constraint.* For each of the 14 targets (`TARGETS`): a domain (counties, regions, a box, or the 40 km around a line's placed ends), a side (export: SF > 0), and the evidence for every regional pattern: precision (share of the pattern's |SF| >= 0.3 mass inside the domain), side share, stations covered, and three tests; the grade uses (i), the others are reported beside it: (i) the pattern's activity recovered in 10 Sep - 10 Oct (hour-by-hour non-negative least squares with the summer loadings) against the target's published day-ahead shadow price, after removing the hour-of-day mean (grade A at r >= 0.6, B at 0.4-0.6, D below 0.4 when at least 20 bound hours exist; C if there is nothing to test); (ii) IMM July timing (share of July the element bound against the pattern's share of July hours with a pole price >= $1); (iii) the correlation of the pattern's shift factors with the Phase 2 regression of every fitted constraint. Line targets use a pocket test instead of a region.
6. *Scale.* The pole scale is the default (`scale_method = pole`: SF = 1 at the pole, the shadow price is the price at the pole). It is replaced by a physical scale only when the published Sep-Oct series tracks the pattern at r >= 0.6 and the slope is a possible shift factor (0.03 to 1): that happened for E_PASP only (slope 0.099; the IMM-rent route gives 0.157). The NELRIO slope is 1.61 and WESTEX 1.46 (rent route 1.47): above 1 means the pattern also carries co-binding constraints, so the pole scale stays. The product SF x mu is the same on either scale; the scale matters for saturation (relief / limit) and for the screens that require |SF| >= 0.10, so both are written (`sf`, `sf_pole`; `sph_usd_mw_yr`, `sph_pole_usd_mw_yr`).
7. *Annual target.* Window sum of the pole shadow price x 8,760 / 1,008 x a season factor from the IMM (median 2026 month over the window months; 1.0 without a series). The `sph` of WESTEX ($17k/MW-yr at the pole) implies $183M/yr on the 10,629 MW limit against $155M annualised DA rent in 2026 and $89-127M in 2025: same order. *Driver*: Phase 3b's physics rule over the 13 hub/zone spreads, fitted on days 1-32 and tested on days 33-42.

## 5. Caveats

1. **Day-ahead, summer, one window.** No real-time data, no spring, no winter. Loadings explain 40% of the standardised Sep-Oct basis. The season factor uses the IMM's median 2026 month where one exists (WESTEX 2.2, E_PASP 0.79, NELRIO 0.98) and 1.0 elsewhere.
2. **The constraint behind a pattern is inferred.** Statistical patterns mix co-binding constraints (WESTEX with 35100__A; the Panhandle with Scurry-Knapp at night). Names are graded; grade D is stored under an `R1_` id.
3. **Physical scale is known for one pattern.** E_PASP: 0.099 (published slope), 0.157 (IMM rent), about 0.3 from Phase 2's fit: a factor of three. The others are on the pole scale; saturation (relief / limit) will overstate relief on NELRIO, WESTEX and the Far West until a scale is chosen.
4. **Interpolated sites.** 4 of the 41 picks are interpolated; 38 of the 40 existing candidates are (median 10.6 km from the nearest priced station, maximum 40.9). Use `dist_priced_km` and `basis_source`; a narrow pocket (NELRIO's Kenedy-Willacy stations) is under-represented at interpolated sites.
5. **Placement.** A wrong coordinate gives a wrong region tag and a wrong neighbour. G1's fixes are in; 67 resource nodes have no coordinates at all.
6. The first-order upgrade numbers use Phase 4's capture factor 0.205 and residuals (0.10 wires, 0.50 interface); they are exposures, not valuations.
