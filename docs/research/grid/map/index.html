Where to put batteries on the ERCOT grid

Batter Up research, 11 Oct 2026. Public data only. A 2 h battery, energy only, earns different money at different substations; this map shows where, and what would change it.

How to read this map

Green circles are the 40 substations where a 2 h battery earns the most in our model. Bigger = more $/kW-yr (learner, 2021–25 mean). Darker = a bigger gain over what the same battery earns at its load-zone price. Click one for its whole row.

Diamonds are the regional candidates in the Panhandle, the Valley, the Far West, Laredo/South and the Bell County pocket (picked from measured summer 2026 prices, valued like the 40 but never ranked with them). The basis layer shows those measured prices: where a node traded below (amber) or above (blue) its load zone. In this window the Valley and Panhandle trade below their zone, an export discount a battery does not gain from.

Lines are the transmission constraints that bind most (thicker = higher priority; dashed = did not bind in our week of data). A blue end is the import side: the price rises when the line binds, so a battery sells there. An amber end is the export side: the price falls, so a battery charges there and, injecting, loads the line.

Electrical distance, not hop count, decides value: a substation 8 hops away can be worth more than one at the end of the line. Values hold for the first ~10 MW behind a line only.

Every number carries a label: REAL a named source, DERIVED computed from real numbers, SIM a model output, ASSUMPTION our choice. Node prices are SIM: levels are good to about a factor of 2, rankings are more stable. Market value and reliability value are never added.
